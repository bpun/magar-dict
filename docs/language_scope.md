**Status:** DRAFT. Every statement in Section 2 must be confirmed by the

## 1. Purpose

This document defines which language varieties the dictionary covers, how
they are written, what counts as a "form," and what is out of scope. It
is the reference for every data, validation, and UI decision.

## 2. Varieties in Scope

The v1.0 dictionary covers three Magar varieties: **Dhut**, **Kham**, and
**Kaike**. Each is a dialect tag in the system.

| Variety (tag slug) | Where it is traditionally spoken* | Notes for the team* | Verifier status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dhut (`dhut`) | TO CONFIRM (western hill districts) | TO CONFIRM | Open |
| Kham (`kham`) | TO CONFIRM (mid-western hill districts) | Has internal sub-varieties; see 2.2 | Open |
| Kaike (`kaike`) | TO CONFIRM (north-western high-hill area) | Small speaker population; treat data with extra care | Open |

*Filled in by the language lead with each verifier. Do not publish
geographic or population claims that no verifier has confirmed.

### 2.1 Naming
- Names are written as the communities write them. Spellings of "Dhut,"
  "Kham," and "Kaike" in Latin and Devanagari are confirmed with the
  verifiers and recorded here: ______
- Whether to describe these as "dialects," "varieties," or "languages" in
  the public interface is decided by the verifiers (OD-6). The database
  tag kind stays `dialect`; the displayed label is a setting.

### 2.2 Sub-varieties
Some varieties have regional sub-varieties. v1.0 stores **one standard
form per variety** and records the village or area of the speaker in
`word_forms.region_note` (optional). Sub-variety tagging is a v1.1 topic.

### 2.3 Shared words
A word is "shared" when the verifiers agree that it is used in all three
varieties with an equivalent meaning. Shared status is **decided by the
verifiers, not inferred from similar spelling or by AI**.

## 3. Scripts and Romanization

| Item | v1.0 decision |
|---|---|
| Primary display script | Devanagari |
| Romanization | Included for every form, using one documented scheme per variety |
| Storage | Both stored separately in `word_forms` (`form_script`, `form_romanized`) |
| Unicode | All text stored as UTF-8, normalized to NFC |
| Search | Both fields are searchable; a normalized romanization is also stored |
| Font | Self-hosted Noto Sans Devanagari with fallback stack |

### 3.1 Romanization scheme
The language lead documents here, per variety:
- the scheme used (for example IAST-based, or a community orthography)
- how tone, aspiration, nasalization, and vowel length are written
- 10 worked examples per variety

One scheme per variety. Mixed schemes inside one variety fail validation.

### 3.2 IPA
IPA is optional in v1.0. When present it is entered by a verifier or a
linguist, never generated by AI.

### 3.3 Akkha script (deferred)
Some Kham communities also use a script called Akkha. The language lead
confirms (a) whether the community wants it supported, and (b) its current
Unicode status. If it is not encodable as text, it cannot be searchable
text in v1.0. Decision: ______ (deferred to v1.1 unless the community
asks for it earlier).

## 4. What a Dictionary Entry Contains

- **Word (language-neutral):** English gloss, Nepali gloss, part of speech,
  source, license.
- **Form (one per variety):** Devanagari spelling, romanization, optional
  IPA, optional audio, verifier, status.
- **Senses:** numbered meanings with English and Nepali text.
- **Examples:** a sentence in the variety with an English translation,
  marked human or AI, and approved before display.
- **Tags:** variety tags (required), topic, level, source.

## 5. Parts of Speech (controlled list, v1.0)
noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, numeral, postposition, particle,
interjection, other. The language lead may adjust this list; the validator
reads the list from one place.

## 6. Out of Scope for v1.0
- Other Magar varieties
- Full grammar reference (the dictionary gives brief grammar notes only)
- Sub-variety (village-level) tagging
- Machine translation of sentences between Magar and other languages
- Speech recognition
- Sacred, ritual, or restricted vocabulary (see Section 7)

## 7. Cultural Sensitivity
Some vocabulary may be restricted to particular rituals, clans, or
occasions. Verifiers may mark any entry as **withheld**. Withheld
entries are never imported, displayed, embedded, or sent to an AI
provider. The import validator checks for a `withheld` flag.

## 8. Data Sources
Each source is listed in `data/SOURCES.md` with its variety, license, and
permission record ID. A source covering one variety must not be used to
populate another.
